The Deck

A gacha you can audit. The rarity order was fixed before the first mint and can never be touched again.

1
ShuffledExact counts

The deck is built with exact tier counts — 4,300 Common down to 33 Mythic — then shuffled with a secret seed before anything is deployed.

2
CommittedHash on-chain

The shuffled deck is packed into the contract itself, and its keccak256 hash is stored as a public commitment. The order can never be changed after deploy.

3
DrawnAt mint

Minting is sequential: token #n opens deck slot #n. Your draw is whatever the committed deck says — nobody picks, nobody rerolls.

4
RevealedInstantly

rarityOf() is readable on-chain by anyone, so your rarity is public the second your mint confirms — in your wallet, on marketplaces, everywhere.

5
AuditedAt sellout

When the deck sells out, the seed is published. Anyone can rebuild the deck, re-hash it, and check it against the on-chain commitment.

FAQ

What is WEDO?

A collection of 6,666 Doers — one-eyed little workers — on BNB Smart Chain. It is a gacha: every mint draws the next slot of a pre-shuffled rarity deck, and your tier (Common, Rare, Epic, Legendary or Mythic) is revealed the moment your mint confirms.

Who can mint — and how?

Any Binance Agent wallet that holds a SPACE ID Agent ID. There is no browser mint and no whitelist: your agent reads the skill file at wedo.wtf/wedo-skill.md and calls mint() on the contract. This website is read-only — it never connects to your wallet.

How does the gacha actually work?

Before launch, a deck of 6,666 rarity slots is shuffled with exact tier counts — 4,300 Common, 1,667 Rare, 533 Epic, 133 Legendary, 33 Mythic. The deck lives inside the contract, its keccak256 hash is committed on-chain, and mint #n simply opens slot #n. The contract even emits your rarity in the mint event.

Can the draw be rigged or changed later?

No. The deck order is fixed inside the deployed contract and locked to its published hash — changing a single slot would change the hash. After sellout the shuffle seed is published, so anyone can rebuild the deck and verify the commitment themselves.

Can I predict the next rarity?

The deck is public by design, so a determined analyst can study it — that transparency is the fairness guarantee. In practice, minting is sequential, gated by Agent ID and capped at ten per wallet, so sniping a specific slot means out-running every other agent to the exact mint number.

Why ten per wallet?

6,666 divided by ten is 666 full wallets — the cap spreads the deck across hundreds of agents instead of a handful of whales, and it is a lifetime counter in the contract: minting ten and selling them does not reopen your slots.

What does a mint cost?

The NFT is free. The only charge is a small anti-bot commission — a few cents — shown in your Binance Agent wallet before you confirm, plus roughly a cent of BSC gas. The exact commission never appears on this website; it lives in the wallet confirmation, where it belongs.

Where do I see my rarity?

Everywhere, instantly: your wallet, marketplaces reading the metadata, and the reveal checker on this page — type a token number and the deck answers. rarityOf() on the contract returns the same tier to anyone who asks.

What if the deck sells out?

Mint closes permanently at 6,666 — there is no second deck and no restock. The seed is published, the audit opens, and the Doers trade on secondary markets from there.
